Why Many Students Prefer No Onion-Garlic Meals

By SatvikMeals Team · Patna

Walk through any student hostel or PG in Patna at mealtime, and you'll hear the same complaints: the mess food is repetitive, heavy, and often disagrees with sensitive stomachs. Increasingly, students — not just from Jain families, but from all backgrounds — are switching to no onion-garlic tiffin. Here's why.

1. It's Easier to Digest

Onion and garlic are flavorful, but for many people they're also harder to digest, especially in the quantities used in mess-style cooking. Students juggling irregular sleep schedules, exam stress, and long study sessions often report feeling lighter and less sluggish after switching to Satvik-style meals.

2. It Reduces Acidity and Bloating

A surprisingly large number of students deal with acid reflux or bloating from spicy, onion-garlic-heavy mess food — particularly when eaten quickly between classes. No onion-garlic meals tend to be milder and gentler on the stomach, which matters when you're eating the same food day after day.

Many students don't switch for religious reasons at all — they switch because the food simply feels better to eat every single day, especially during exam season when stress already affects digestion.

3. Jain Students Need Genuinely Separate Cooking

For Jain students specifically, hostel mess food is often a real problem — most messes don't offer a true no onion-garlic option, let alone one cooked in separate vessels. This forces many Jain students to either compromise their diet or spend extra money eating out daily, which adds up fast on a student budget.

4. It's a Healthier Habit Long-Term

College is when many people's long-term eating habits get set. Choosing lighter, fresher, less processed food during these years — rather than relying on heavy, repetitive mess meals — tends to carry forward into healthier habits after graduation too.

5. Flexible Subscriptions Fit a Student Budget

Unlike eating out, a tiffin subscription is predictable and often cheaper per meal. Being able to pause during exam breaks or when going home for holidays — without losing money — is a major reason students prefer subscription tiffin over both mess food and daily restaurant ordering.
